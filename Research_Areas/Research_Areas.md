# Research Areas

My work sits at the intersection of microbiology, intensive care medicine, and computational biology. I’m particularly interested in how molecular mechanisms, host–pathogen interactions, and data‑driven analysis can be combined to understand and improve outcomes in critical illness.

---

## Microbiology & Infection Biology
I focus on mechanistic microbiology, including:
- quorum sensing and anti‑virulence strategies  
- biofilm formation and device‑associated infections  
- pathogen–host interactions in critical care  
- antimicrobial resistance and its clinical implications  

This includes both wet‑lab concepts and computational approaches to understanding microbial behaviour.

---

## Intensive Care & Clinical Microbiology
I’m interested in how infection biology translates into real‑world ICU settings, especially:
- ventilator‑associated pneumonia  
- sepsis and bacteraemia  
- burn wound infections  
- the role of virulence factors in acute deterioration  

My work often explores how emerging therapies or diagnostics could be integrated into critical care practice.

---

## Computational Biology & Bioinformatics
I work across a range of computational methods, including:
- sequencing data QC and preprocessing  
- alignment, variant calling, and VCF interpretation  
- SAM/BAM literacy and HPC workflows  
- early‑stage RNA‑seq and metagenomics concepts  

This includes my ongoing **Bioinformatics Crash Course**, where I document my learning and build practical tools.

---

## Data Science & Statistical Genetics
I’m developing skills in:
- genome‑wide association studies (GWAS)  
- population structure correction  
- mixed‑model association testing  
- visualisation and interpretation of genetic signals  

This area connects my interest in infection biology with host genetics and computational epidemiology.

---

## Science Communication & Misinformation
I also work on the social side of biomedical science, including:
- infectious‑disease misinformation  
- AMR communication and public understanding  
- how scientific language shapes public narratives  
- ethical responsibilities in biomedical communication  

This includes my commentary on AMR misinformation and xenophobic discourse.
